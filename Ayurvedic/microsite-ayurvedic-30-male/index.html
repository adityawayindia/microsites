When the working day ends

The journey home, evening meal and last tasks of the day can all be part of your consultation.

On some days there is a long commute, on others there are caring responsibilities or evening work. Point out the part of this transition that you would most like to discuss. He also asks about current medicines and recommendations from other clinicians, so these are included in the discussion. Before you leave, you can ask which details to write down, what to bring next time and whether an in-person examination is needed.

Helpful answers

Before your visit

Answers to common questions about your consultation and follow-up.

Many people find warm water comforting for digestion, and it is a harmless habit for most. People with specific medical conditions should follow their doctor's fluid advice.

Regular sleep, meal timing, stress reduction and trigger avoidance are often emphasised. Sudden severe headache, vision changes or weakness need emergency medical care.

Frequent breaks, posture correction, gentle stretches and warm oil application are commonly advised. Pain radiating to the arm or with numbness should be checked by a specialist.

Poorly made products can contain unsafe levels of metals, so buy only from licensed manufacturers and use them under guidance. Stop and seek medical help if you notice unusual symptoms.

Lifestyle, diet and stress support may ease hot flushes, sleep trouble and mood changes for some women. Heavy bleeding or bleeding after menopause needs a gynaecologist's review.

Eat freshly cooked meals at regular times, in a calm setting and in a quantity that leaves you comfortably light. Avoid combining heavy, oily food with late night eating.

Online consultations are governed by the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020. They are not for emergencies, and the doctor may advise an in-person visit where an examination is needed.
