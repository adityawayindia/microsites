About the doctor

Small steps.
Thoughtful conversations.

Pediatric care with time for your child and the questions on your mind.

Dr. Tara Mehta is a pediatrician who makes space for the child’s story and the family’s questions. She begins with the concerns that brought you in, then reviews growth, development, daily routines and relevant medical records. At Mint Circle Pediatrics, visits can include newborn and infant reviews, vaccination planning, nutrition and feeding discussions, common childhood illnesses and school-age health. Examinations and investigations are discussed when appropriate, and the findings are explained in plain language. Families are encouraged to bring previous prescriptions, reports and vaccination records so care stays connected between visits. Each consultation ends with a discussion of the next steps, what to watch for and when to follow up. Online consultations are available for suitable concerns when enabled by the practice; some children will need an in-person examination.

Helpful answers

Before your visit

Answers to common questions about children’s health.

Offer home cooked meals, limit sugary drinks and snacks and encourage an hour of play daily. Family habits matter most.

Constant restlessness, trouble focusing and impulsive behaviour beyond what is expected for age may suggest ADHD. Early assessment guides support at home and school.

Lack of vitamin D, calcium or sunlight softens bones, causing bowed legs and delayed walking. Supplements and sunlight prevent and treat it.

Measles can cause pneumonia, brain swelling and death. Two doses give strong lifelong protection.

Fast breathing, wheeze and feeding difficulty after a cold suggest it. Babies who breathe hard or feed poorly need quick medical care.

Begin as soon as the first tooth appears, using a rice grain sized smear of fluoride paste. Limit sweets and avoid bottles at bedtime to prevent cavities.

Online consultations are governed by the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020. They are not for emergencies, and the doctor may advise an in-person visit where an examination is needed.
