Helpful answers

Before your visit

Answers to common questions about children’s health.

Lay the child on their side, keep the area safe and do not put anything in the mouth. Most are brief and harmless, but seek care after the first one.

No babbling by 12 months or few words by 18 to 24 months should be checked. Hearing tests and early therapy bring big gains.

Hives, vomiting, swelling or breathing trouble soon after eating suggest allergy. Avoid the trigger and get emergency help for breathing difficulty.

A viral illness with fever, mouth ulcers and blisters on hands and feet, common in young children. It settles in about a week with fluids and rest.

Most children outgrow them. Surgery is considered only for very frequent, severe infections despite treatment.

Toddlers need 11 to 14 hours and school children 9 to 11 hours. A fixed bedtime routine without screens helps sleep.

Online consultations are governed by the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 2020. They are not for emergencies, and the doctor may advise an in-person visit where an examination is needed.
