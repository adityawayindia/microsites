About the doctor

Reviewing chest CT findings

Ask which older examination was used when a report describes a finding as stable.

Dr. Ishaan Talwar is a radiologist at Northbridge Imaging Practice in Kochi. Chest CT can address a range of referral questions. A comparison with the right earlier scan can be especially helpful if the report describes a small finding in the lung. Bring the prior report and access to the original images when possible. Your radiologist can explain what has been compared and what the conclusion says. He can go through the report in the order that makes sense to you. For example, you might want to start with its conclusion, check what was compared and then discuss the details that prompted the recommendation. You can bring a short list of questions and agree what to take back to the clinician who requested the examination.

Before your visit

Answers to common questions about your consultation and follow-up.

Ultrasound is often the first test for a lump in younger women with dense breast tissue, and it shows whether a lump is solid or fluid filled. Mammography is usually added in older women, and a biopsy may be needed if the lump looks suspicious.

MRI is the best test for discs, nerves and the spinal cord because it shows soft tissue clearly. X-rays show bone alignment and CT is used when fine bone detail is needed.

Echocardiography uses ultrasound to see heart size, pumping and valves. CT coronary angiography or cardiac MRI can be used to look at the arteries and heart muscle in more detail.

Some centres ask for a few hours of fasting before contrast CT, while others do not. Follow the instructions you were given and drink fluids as advised.
