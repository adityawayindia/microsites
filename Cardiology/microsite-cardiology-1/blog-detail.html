Understanding Your Heart: A Guide to Common Cardiac Conditions

Dr. Arjun Mehta

Interventional Cardiologist

Updated on 20 Jul 2025

The human heart is a remarkable organ, beating approximately 100,000 times a day to circulate blood throughout your body. Yet heart disease remains the leading cause of death worldwide, including in India, where cardiovascular diseases account for nearly one-third of all deaths. The good news is that most cardiac conditions are highly manageable — and even preventable — with early detection and the right treatment.

In this article, I will walk you through the most common cardiac conditions I see in my practice at HeartCare Clinic, their key symptoms, and why prompt diagnosis makes all the difference.

1. Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)

Coronary artery disease is the most prevalent form of heart disease. It occurs when the arteries supplying blood to the heart muscle become narrowed or blocked due to a build-up of plaques — a process known as atherosclerosis. Over time, this restricts blood flow to the heart, leading to chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, and in severe cases, a heart attack. Risk factors include high cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, obesity, and a sedentary lifestyle.

2. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

Often called the "silent killer", hypertension rarely causes obvious symptoms until it has already caused significant damage to the heart, kidneys, or blood vessels. Blood pressure above 130/80 mmHg is considered elevated. Long-term uncontrolled hypertension forces the heart to work harder, leading to left ventricular hypertrophy, heart failure, and increased stroke risk. Regular blood pressure checks are essential for everyone above the age of 30.

3. Heart Failure

Heart failure does not mean the heart has stopped — it means the heart is not pumping blood as effectively as it should. Symptoms include persistent fatigue, swelling in the legs and ankles, shortness of breath (especially when lying flat), and a reduced ability to exercise. Heart failure can result from CAD, hypertension, diabetes, or previous heart attacks. Modern treatment with medications and devices has dramatically improved outcomes for heart failure patients.

4. Arrhythmias

Arrhythmias are abnormal heart rhythms. The heart may beat too fast (tachycardia), too slow (bradycardia), or irregularly. Atrial fibrillation (AFib) is the most common arrhythmia and significantly increases the risk of stroke. Patients often describe palpitations, dizziness, lightheadedness, or fainting. An ECG or Holter monitor study is essential for diagnosis.

5. Valvular Heart Disease

The heart has four valves that ensure blood flows in one direction. Rheumatic heart disease (a common cause in India), calcification, and congenital defects can damage these valves, causing them to become either narrowed (stenosis) or leaky (regurgitation). Symptoms may include shortness of breath, fatigue, and heart murmur on examination. Echocardiography is the gold standard for diagnosis.

When Should You See a Cardiologist?

You should seek a cardiac evaluation if you experience: persistent chest discomfort or pressure, unexplained breathlessness, palpitations or an irregular heartbeat, dizziness or fainting episodes, swelling in your legs, or if you have a family history of heart disease. Early intervention is always better than crisis management.
