Managing Severe Sepsis: Protocols and ICU Care

Dr. Sarah Jenkins

Intensivist Doctor

Updated on 12 Jun 2026

Sepsis is a life-threatening medical emergency caused by the body's systemic response to an infection. In the Intensive Care Unit (ICU), early detection and structured protocols are vital to halt its progression into severe sepsis or septic shock.

ICU Sepsis Bundles

Intensivist-led teams implement the Hour-1 bundle immediately upon suspicion of sepsis. This includes measuring lactate levels, obtaining blood cultures, administering broad-spectrum antibiotics, and initiating rapid fluid resuscitation. If arterial pressure remains low, vasopressors are introduced to support perfusion.

Sepsis management in the ICU is a race against time; every hour of delay in administering appropriate antibiotics correlates with a measurable decrease in survival rates.

Continuous Telemetry Monitoring

Throughout recovery, the patient undergoes continuous telemetry, arterial line monitoring, and urine output tracking to assess cardiac output and kidney function. Care plans are constantly refined based on blood gases, lactate clearance, and clinical response.

If you or your family members are seeking details on post-ICU recovery and sepsis follow-up protocols, consulting with the intensive care team will help establish a structured rehabilitation plan.
